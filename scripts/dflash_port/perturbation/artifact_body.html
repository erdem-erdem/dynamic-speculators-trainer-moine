Move vs Improve

How far can the drafter's predictions be pushed by changing its weights, where, and does pushing them ever bring them closer to the target model? A perturbation study on 100 stored validation rows, with gradients and training only on the 24K training rows.

Answer

Hard to improve, not hard to move.

Every part of the drafter can be moved, and random changes never help. Across 1,100 random weight perturbations from the base drafter, the best Δτ is +0.004, inside the bfloat16 noise floor. Trained changes move the drafter far more per unit of weight: the 24K static changes attention weights by 2.9% and flips 15.8% of draft tokens. What that buys is a small net of two large flows: 2.84% of positions fixed and 2.21% broken. Every improving change found, from a single gradient step to a full fine-tune, sits on one concave front of gain against movement that flattens near +0.2 τ. Full fine-tuning, rank 512 and FFN adapters all end at the same place.

Setup

What was perturbed, and how it is measured

Each experiment takes a starting point θ₁ and changes some of its weights by ΔW to get θ₂. Both run on the same stored contexts and the same anchored 16-token blocks, and the page compares their next-token distributions at each of the 15 draft slots.

  • Starting points. The released base drafter, and the base drafter with the 24K static LoRA (KL loss, rank 32 on attention, coefficient 2) applied exactly as trained.
  • Movement is how far θ₂'s predictions are from θ₁'s: KL(p₁‖p₂) per draft position, total variation, and the share of positions whose top token changes (flips).
  • Gain is whether the move helps: Δτ (change in accepted tokens per block, greedy, teacher-forced), Δ accuracy, and the change in KL to the target's distribution.
  • Churn splits the accuracy change into fixes (wrong before, right after) and breaks (right before, wrong after).
  • Kinds of ΔW. Random dense Gaussian at a relative size ε = ‖ΔW‖/‖W‖ (T1), random low-rank at the same size, one step along the training gradient or against it (T0), short trainings of 600 updates (T2), and the existing trained adapters as free data points.

Gradients and training use 24K training rows only (1,000 per category for gradients); the 100 evaluation rows are a seeded sample of the 10K-math validation split. The loss everywhere is KL(p_T‖p) with D-PACE position weights, as in the 24K runs. All numbers are offline, greedy and teacher-forced; “all” is the mean over the four categories. On all 500 validation rows the harness reproduces the recorded 24K static score exactly (0.53379) and the base score to within 3·10⁻⁶ (0.526526 vs 0.526529).

Movement against gain

Every perturbation is one point: how far it moved the predictions (x, log scale) and what it did to τ (y). The black step line is the Pareto front, the best gain found at or below each amount of movement.

F3All perturbations and trained adapters

Points below −0.45 τ are drawn at the bottom edge. Hover any point for its location, size, seed and churn.

Random noise falls off the bottom; only gradient directions climb. From base, the random points (blue) stay at or below zero and fall faster the further they move. Every point on the front above the floor is a gradient step, a training checkpoint or a trained adapter. The front rises with log-movement and flattens: +0.04 τ at KL 0.01 (one step on attention), +0.10 at 0.03 (full fine-tune, 150 updates), +0.18 at 0.16 (24K static), +0.20 at 0.24 (24K generator, its own LoRA per row). One gradient step overshoots quickly. Three times the best step on attention turns +0.10 into −0.29, and at all 220 location-and-size pairs from base the ascent step costs more τ than the descent step of the same size gains.

Switch to θ₁ = 24K static. The first-order gain is used up: the mean gradient is 4.5 times smaller on attention than at base, and the best single step anywhere gains +0.011 τ (layer 4 context path), inside the noise. Random noise around the static point behaves as it does around base.

T1 · F1

Dose-response: random weight noise

Dense Gaussian ΔW at five sizes, two seeds each; the band spans the two seeds. The dotted line is the floor measured at ε = 10⁻⁴, where any change upstream re-rounds the bfloat16 forward and flips near-tied top tokens.

F1Response against relative size

Every location moves, and noise never helps. With enough noise every location moves the predictions. Groups move first: the whole drafter reaches KL 0.01 at ε ≈ 0.05 and fc at ε ≈ 0.06, while a single module usually needs ε ≈ 0.3–0.9. Only two locations stay below KL 0.01 even at ε = 1: q of layer 1 and the block path of layer 1's K/V. The two seeds agree so closely that the bands are hard to see. Switch the metric to Δτ: random noise only costs, and the cost grows with the size of the move.

The floor matters for reading small effects. At ε = 10⁻⁴ the predictions already differ by KL 3.7·10⁻⁴ and 1.1% flips: bfloat16 re-rounding flips top tokens that were near-ties. Below that floor, differences are numerical, not learned.

V, O and the context path move the drafter; layer 1's query and block path barely do. At ε = 0.3, layer 1's V and its K/V context path give the largest single-module KL in attention (0.010), while layer 1's q (0.0009) and block path (0.0014) give the smallest. Fixes and breaks

Accuracy change split into positions that became right and positions that became wrong, against the target's top token.

F4Churn per perturbation (% of draft positions, θ₁ = base)

Gains are a small difference of large flows. The 24K static fixes 2.84% of draft positions and breaks 2.21%, a net of +0.63 points. Every trained point breaks about four positions for every five it fixes. Random noise at a similar movement breaks more than it fixes. The floor bar shows what numerical noise alone churns (0.14% fixed, 0.17% broken).

F5

Per-slot profiles

Movement and accuracy change at each of the 15 draft slots, θ₁ = base.

F5Slots 1–15

Late slots move freely; early slots stay put, and they are the ones that count. Random noise at ε = 0.3 on attention flips 4.8% of slot-1 tokens and 26% at slot 15. The per-slot Fisher trace of the whole drafter rises about eightfold from slot 1 to slot 7. Trained adapters behave the same way: the 24K static fixes 0.89% of positions at slot 1 and 3.8% at slot 15. But slot k only adds to τ when every earlier slot was accepted. Under base, 87% of blocks get slot 1 right and only 19% carry a correct run to slot 15. So the drafter is easiest to move exactly where a change matters least.

T1 · F6

Does rank matter at a fixed size?

Random ΔW = B·A at ranks 32 to 1024, scaled so ‖ΔW‖ equals the dense perturbation's; 4096 marks the dense Gaussian. Solid lines ε = 0.1, dashed ε = 0.3.

F6Movement against rank

Rank does not limit movement. At a fixed ‖ΔW‖, a random rank-32 ΔW moves the predictions as much as a dense one: From rank 32 to dense, KL agrees within 4% on all attention, all FFN and layer 5, within 11% on layer 1 (low rank slightly lower) and within 15% on fc, at both sizes. Whatever caps a rank-32 adapter, it is not that low-rank changes cannot reach the outputs.

T2 · F7

Training paths

Three 600-update trainings from the base drafter (KL + D-PACE, 18 rows per update), evaluated at updates 150, 300 and 600, on the F3 axes. Stars are the existing 3-epoch adapters; the dashed green line is the best a single gradient step reached.

F7Movement and gain over training

Every way of training ends near the same place. The two rank-512 LoRAs reach +0.13 τ within 150 updates and then gain little: +0.148 (attention) and +0.142 (FFN) at 600 updates. The full fine-tune of all 1.05B linear weights moves the predictions less at its smaller learning rate (KL 0.086) and gains +0.135. On all 500 validation rows the three reach τ 7.375 (full), 7.397 (attention LoRA) and 7.393 (FFN LoRA), against 7.403 for the 3-epoch rank-32 static and 7.223 for base. More parameters, higher rank, or FFN instead of attention do not raise the ceiling in 600 updates.

None of these trainings lowers its own objective on held-out rows. On the 100 sweep rows the KL + D-PACE loss rises from 0.421 (base) to 0.432–0.436 for every trained point, and plain KL to the target from 1.85 to 1.90–1.95 nats per position, while τ rises. The 24K static run's logged training loss also never fell (0.418 in its first 100 updates, 0.431 near update 3,600). Upstream documents the D-PACE weights for a cross-entropy per-position loss, and here they are computed from KL; whether that explains the mismatch is open.

Do categories want different changes?

One descent step of size ε = 3·10⁻⁴ along the gradient of one category's training rows, evaluated on every category. Cells show Δτ on each category's 25 rows (noisy: one category is a quarter of the sweep). If the diagonal dominates, categories want different directions and a per-prompt adapter has room. The table gives cosines between the categories' mean gradients next to the ceiling that gradient noise alone allows.

F8Cross-category gain

From base, categories mostly agree. The four categories' mean gradients point the same way: on all attention, the pairwise cosines are 0.58–0.82, 0.71–0.87 after dividing by the ceiling that gradient noise allows. A step on a category's own gradient still helps that category more. On all attention it gains +0.058 τ on its own rows, against +0.031 from the other categories' steps and +0.041 from the shared step. A per-category direction is worth about +0.02 τ over a shared one at first order, the same size as the 24K generator's measured margin over the static (+0.022).

From the static point, there is little left to split. The mean gradient is 4.5 times smaller on attention, and 1,000 rows per category no longer resolve its direction: the noise ceiling for a category pair falls to 0.08–0.10. Category steps gain nothing measurable (+0.003 τ own, −0.002 other, +0.003 shared on attention). Whatever per-prompt room is left after a static adapter is below what this test can see.

F9Sensitivity and gain by module

Read with F2: V, O and the context path are the most movable parts of attention, layer 1's query and block path the least, and FFN sensitivity grows with depth. fc moves the drafter most per relative change. Implications

What this means for adapters and the generator

The limit is the direction, not capacity or placement. Adding parameters, rank or FFN targets does not raise the ceiling, and a static adapter already takes most of the first-order gain, including most of what categories share. The next experiments should change what is optimized and how far each step goes, not how big the adapter is.

  • Make the objective track τ (suggestion D3). Every trained point raises its own KL + D-PACE loss on held-out rows while τ rises. A τ-aligned loss, or D-PACE weights computed from a cross-entropy confidence as upstream documents, should be tested first with three 600-update runs, checking that loss and τ then move together.
  • Weight the early slots. Slots 1–4 decide most of τ but move least; the movable late slots rarely count. Re-weight the loss toward early slots, or give early slots their own adapter capacity.
  • Control the step. Gradient steps overshoot at three times the best size. A learning-rate sweep for the full fine-tune (D5's alternative: 10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁵, 5·10⁻⁵), and a trust-region or line-searched update for the static, test whether the front can be followed further.
  • Keep the generator small and on top of the static (D1, G1, A1). From base, the per-category first-order margin is about +0.02 τ, the generator's measured margin. A basis-LoRA mixture with the static as LoRA 0, or routed per-category residuals, should match it at a fraction of 1.1B parameters. Expect no more than that.
  • Do not spend on capacity. Full fine-tuning, rank 512 and FFN targets end at the same τ in 600 updates; rank does not limit how far a ΔW can move the outputs.
Limits

What these numbers do not show

  • One seed per training run. T2 runs and the existing adapters are single seeds; earlier reports put seed-to-seed drift at the size of the differences between T2 runs.
  • Offline, greedy, teacher-forced. τ here is the accepted run against the target's greedy token on stored responses, not served speed or sampled acceptance.
  • 100 rows. A Δτ interval is about ±0.04 wide; per-category numbers rest on 25 rows each and are noisier still. The 500-row validation is given for the T2 end points.
  • One step size grid per kind. Gradient steps use relative sizes 10⁻⁴ to 3·10⁻³, random noise 10⁻² to 1. The first gradient grid (3·10⁻³ to 0.1) destroyed fc and every group and was stopped after 20 rows; it is kept aside, not used.
  • From the static point, gradient steps cover fc, the K/V paths, the groups and the layers, not single q/o/FFN modules (time budget).
  • Movement is measured from θ₁ only. Training paths start from base; the existing adapters differ in data and loss (24K KL, 10K-math CE, 4K CE), so they are reference points, not a controlled comparison.
