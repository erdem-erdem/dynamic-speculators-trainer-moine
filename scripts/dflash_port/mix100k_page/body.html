MIX100K Static vs Generator

The two strongest designs from the 24K ablations, scaled to 100,000 training rows: a rank-512 static LoRA (68M parameters) against a generator that makes a rank-64 LoRA for every prompt (≈ 0.45B parameters, heads shared across layers). Same loss, schedule and evaluation rows; the 24K runs are shown for context.

Settings

What the two arms are

Both adapt the same frozen drafter with LoRA on q, k, v and o of all five layers at coefficient 2. The static arm learns one rank-512 LoRA used for every prompt. The generator reads the prompt's summary (the target's hidden states at the last prompt token) and emits a rank-64 LoRA for that prompt; its heads are shared across the five layers, which kept it 5× smaller than per-layer heads with no measured loss at 24K.

Why these two: at 24K (ABL24K) static rank was the largest lever (r512 +0.046 τ over r32 on test), and every generator landed near +0.03 regardless of size; shared-head r64 was the strongest generator under the 500M target. A hybrid of the two (per-layer static r512 plus shared heads) did not pass at 24K.

Limits

What these numbers do not show

  • One seed per arm; differences below about ±0.015 τ are within the resolution of these 500 rows and of seed drift.
  • Offline, greedy, teacher-forced τ on stored responses; no vLLM serving measurement. The generator adds a per-request LoRA (generation plus merge) that this page does not cost.
  • The 24K and 100K points differ in both data and epochs (2 vs 3).
  • The base drafter was likely trained on prompts from the same Nemotron Post-Training V2 source.
