Where DFlash Moves

The 24K static LoRA adds about 0.2 accepted tokens per block over the released z-lab/Qwen3-8B-DFlash-b16 drafter, and the per-prompt generator adds only 0.02 more. Five measurements on the base, static and generator checkpoints show how much error is left to fix, where in the drafter an adapter can act, whether categories want different updates, and whether the prompt tells the drafter anything it does not already see.

All numbers are offline, teacher-forced, greedy, single seed, from the validation / test rows of the 24K evaluation sets (500 rows each; knock-outs on 200). τ here is pooled over blocks unless marked "category mean"; it is the accepted run plus the bonus token per 16-token block.

The drafter

One block, two paths into every attention layer

The drafter predicts 15 tokens at once from an anchor. Five target layers are concatenated and projected by fc into the context; every draft layer's k_proj/v_proj turn that context into keys and values (the context path), and the same projections turn the block's own anchor and mask tokens into keys and values (the block path). Queries come only from the block. Colours on the diagram show the measured τ loss when each part is knocked out (base drafter).

Knock-out Δτ (base drafter, 200 validation rows): zeroing a layer's attention output, its MLP, or the K/V of one path. Hover a box for the value and its 95% interval.
Step 1 · error decomposition

How much of the remaining error looks fixable

For every block we find the first slot that fails and ask what the target thought there (how confident its top token was) and what the drafter thought (where it ranked the target's token). We also check whether the data stayed on the target's greedy path inside the block: if the sampled response took a non-top token earlier in the block, the later label follows a token the drafter could not have known.

How each block ends (base drafter, test, 49,021 blocks). Rank 1–5 = the drafter had the target's token among its top 5. Rank 1 at a miss is an exact bf16 logit tie that argmax broke the other way.

Step 2 · gradient maps

Where a rank-32 update could help, and where categories disagree

We back-propagated the training loss (KL to the target + D-PACE position weights) through 1,000 training rows per category and accumulated the gradient of every drafter weight, with K and V split by path. Three views per module: how much a rank-32 step can lower the loss to first order; how much of each category's best rank-32 step one shared update captures; and how aligned the categories' gradients are once split-half noise is removed.

Rank-32 first-order gain

‖top-32 SVD of the mean gradient‖, mean over categories (larger = a rank-32 update there lowers the loss faster per unit of weight change).

Captured by one shared update

First-order gain of the best shared rank-32 direction as a share of each category's own best rank-32 direction, both fitted on one half of the rows and scored on the other (1 = a per-category adapter gains nothing over a static one).

Category agreement (noise-corrected)

Cross-category cosine of half-sample gradients divided by the split-half cosine. Near 1: categories want the same update. Low: they disagree (where a dynamic adapter has room).

Split-half reliability

Cosine between gradients of two disjoint halves of the same category. Low values mean the gradient is mostly sampling noise at 500 rows.
Step 3 · prompt usage

Does the drafter need the prompt?

The generator builds its LoRA from the prompt. If the drafter already reads the prompt from its own context, that information is redundant.
