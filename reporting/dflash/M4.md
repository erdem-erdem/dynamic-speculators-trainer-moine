## What is tested

How does scaling the generated (and static) ΔW at inference change acceptance? Does the best coefficient vary per row, which would point towards a dynamic coefficient?

## Methodology

Offline sweep s ∈ {0.5, 1, 2 ★, 4, 8} on the final M0 checkpoints (s replaces the training scale α/r = 2: ΔW_s = (s/2)·ΔW). No training. One LoRA per row (K=1), the 200 fixed validation rows; same rows and anchors for every s. K=4 category means for continuity (comparable only with other K=4 numbers). EAGLE-3 GEN-008 swept s in serving only (s = 2 best; 8 collapsed).

## Measurements

Per s: score (`full_acc`), τ (`eal`), τ per category; K=4 score. Per row: τ at every s, the row's best s, and the gain of a per-row oracle over the best fixed s (mean of row τ, unweighted by anchors, so it differs slightly from the pooled τ).

- The **generator is best at s = 1** (0.5289), i.e. at **half** the scale it was trained with; s = 0.5 is close (0.5285). At its training scale it scores 0.5220. The **static LoRA is best at its training scale** (s = 2: 0.5304; s = 1: 0.5300).
- Large coefficients hurt both, the generator much more: s = 8 gives 0.2161 (generator) vs 0.3096 (static); s = 4: 0.4785 vs 0.5169.
- Consistent with this, the generated ΔW are larger than the static ΔW: median ratio of Frobenius norms ≈ 7× over the 20 modules (1.9× to 13×; static ‖ΔW‖ from its checkpoint, generator from D13's `mean_norm`).
