## What is tested

How different are the LoRAs the generator writes for different prompts, and do they cluster by category? Mirrors the EAGLE-3 GEN-005 analysis (cosine within vs between category, dyn_ratio, effective rank, category accuracy), with our own exact definitions (EAGLE-3's script is not in the repo).

## Methodology

For the 200 validation rows, the generated ΔW_i = s·B_i·A_i per module (s = 2), compared exactly through r×r Gram matrices (no ΔW is materialised). Per module and pooled over all modules:
- **cos_in / cos_cross:** mean cosine between ΔW of rows of the same / different categories; **gap** = their difference;
- **dyn_ratio:** variance of ΔW around its mean over total ‖ΔW‖² (0 = every row gets the same ΔW, as a static LoRA);
- **η²:** share of that variance explained by category; **effective rank** of the centred Gram; **centroid accuracy:** nearest category centroid (leave-one-out) recovers the row's category.

## Results

**M0 generator, all modules pooled (200 LoRAs):** cos within 0.9252, across 0.6667 (gap 0.2585); dyn_ratio 0.2684; η² 0.744; effective rank 3.60; centroid accuracy 0.965.

For reference: after 100 updates (P2 smoke) the same analysis gave dyn_ratio 0.0005 and gap 0.0004 (nearly one LoRA for all rows). A static LoRA has dyn_ratio 0 by construction.

- The generator is clearly **dynamic and category-aware**: 27 % of the ΔW energy varies across prompts, about three quarters of that variation is explained by category, and the category is recoverable from the LoRA for 96.5 % of rows.
- Dynamism is highest in the first draft layer (dyn_ratio 0.39 to 0.50) and lowest in the last layer's v_proj (0.14).
- The generated ΔW are about 7× larger in norm than the matched static LoRA's (median over modules; see M4).
- This dynamism does not improve acceptance at 4K (M0, M7): the generator overfits.
