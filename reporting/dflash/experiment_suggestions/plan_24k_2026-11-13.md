## 1. Why KL does not target τ, and the gating fix

### 1.1 What τ rewards
For one block, with aⱼ ∈ {0, 1} = "slot j is accepted":

```
τ = 1 + Σₖ Πⱼ≤ₖ aⱼ
```

- Only the **leading run** counts: a slot after the first miss is worth nothing.
- Under greedy verification, a slot counts only if the drafter's **argmax** equals the target's argmax. The rest of the distribution is irrelevant.

### 1.2 What KL rewards
KL(p_T ‖ p) differs from τ in three ways:
- **It is per slot and independent.** Slot 12 after a certain miss at slot 2 is weighted as if it mattered.
- **It matches the whole distribution,** including tail mass and entropy. A slot whose argmax is already right can still carry a large KL, and a slot the target is unsure about (p_top < 0.5, 28% of first misses) asks the drafter to copy a spread-out distribution whose argmax is close to a coin flip.
- **Its gradient goes where KL is large,** and that is the late, target-uncertain slots. PERTURB24K showed the movable slots are the late ones, which τ rarely uses.

### 1.3 D-PACE is already the gate, but our KL runs feed it the wrong signal
The smooth expected accepted length, with qⱼ = P(slot j accepted):

```
E[τ] = 1 + Σₖ Πⱼ≤ₖ qⱼ
∂E[τ]/∂θ = Σⱼ wⱼ · ∂ log qⱼ/∂θ,   with  wⱼ = Σₖ≥ⱼ Πᵢ≤ₖ qᵢ
```

- **wⱼ is exactly the D-PACE weight** (`src/speculators/losses/utils.py:64-112`, prefix product then suffix sum), with smoothing α = 0.
- **So CE + D-PACE(α → 0) is the exact gradient of expected accepted length,** when qⱼ is the drafter's probability of the target's token (CE = −log qⱼ). D-PACE is the gating mechanism, and for CE it is the right one.
- **In our KL runs the gate is wrong.** `dpace_loss_decay` computes q = exp(−elementwise_loss) from whatever loss term it is given. With `kl_div` that gives q = exp(−KL): not an acceptance probability, and not related to whether the argmax matches. In `lk_hybrid` each term (KL, TV) builds its own gate the same way.
- **The upstream docstring requires CE** ("elementwise_loss: requires to be cross-entropy loss").
- **The default α = 0.5 floors every factor at 0.5.** After a near-certain miss the gate still passes half the weight to all later slots, so the weighting stays close to uniform.

This is a concrete candidate for PERTURB24K's "the loss rises while τ rises" finding. It is a hypothesis to test (L1 below), not a confirmed cause.

### 1.4 Gated losses to build
One config surface, `block_drafter.gate` (names indicative):

| Knob | Values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `gate_source` | `own` (today) · `label_prob` · `accept_prob` | Where qⱼ comes from (always stop-gradient): today's exp(−loss) · the drafter's probability of the target's argmax (greedy acceptance) · 1 − TV(p, p_T) (sampled acceptance) |
| `dpace_alpha` | 0.5 (today) · 0.1 · 0 | How sharply a likely miss cuts later slots; 0 is the exact τ gradient |
| `slot_term` | KL · CE(target argmax) · CE + λ·KL | What each gated slot optimizes; the KL share keeps the distribution calibrated (useful under sampling, and for the generator's smoothness) |
| `target_conf_gate` | off · weight by p_T(top) · drop p_T(top) < 0.5 | Don't spend gradient on target coin flips |
| `on_path_gate` | off · on | Zero weight after the first earlier slot in the block whose data token differs from the target's argmax (16% of first misses are off-path, with labels from the wrong context) |
| `margin` | off · hinge on (z_target − max other z) at gated slots, m ≈ 1–2 | Flips near-misses directly: 78% of base first misses have the target's token at rank 2–5 |

- Cost: one extra no-grad log-softmax gather per position (for `label_prob`). The fused Triton kernels stay as they are; the gate is computed beside them.
- Tests:
  - zero-gate ≡ uniform;
  - `label_prob` with α = 0 and CE equals autograd of E[τ] on a toy block;
  - today's configuration is unchanged by default.

## 2. Did the base drafter already see our data? Would that make the job harder?

**Very likely yes, partly.**
- z-lab trained the drafter on ≈ 800K samples from **Nemotron Post-Training V2** + CodeAlpaca, with responses generated by the target (paper p6, `notes/05` A.1).
- Our 24K / 100K pools are `openeurollm/Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset-v2-decontaminated` math / code / stem / chat prompts, with Qwen3-8B non-thinking responses.
- The prompts likely overlap in part. The responses are our own samples, so the token sequences differ even where the prompts match. The overlap fraction is unknown: z-lab's 800K subset is not public as far as we know.

**Why it matters (interpretation):**
- **Less headroom for any adapter.** We are fine-tuning on the distribution the drafter was trained on; what is left is mostly a capacity limit, not missing data. This fits the measurements:
  - a flat landscape;
  - the +0.2 τ ceiling shared by full fine-tune, r512 and FFN adapters;
  - categories agreeing on the gradient direction.
- **Less for the generator to add.** Per-prompt or per-category information about Nemotron-style prompts is already in the drafter's weights. ANALYSIS24K also shows category is decodable from every drafter layer.
- **Evaluation looks better for base than a fresh distribution would.** The decontamination checks our splits against each other, not against z-lab's training set.
- **But it is also the conservative test.** A gain here is a gain on the drafter's home turf. A deployed drafter meets prompts it never saw, which is where adaptation should pay.

**What the plan does about it (cheap):**
- **C6, a shift probe (≈ 2–3 h, mostly vLLM generation):**
  - ≈ 200 rows per category of Qwen3-8B **thinking-mode** responses on held-out prompts, plus one non-Nemotron chat source;
  - store-fill and offline-evaluate base / 24K static / 24K generator.
  - The paper reports lower τ in thinking mode (MATH-500 5.8 vs 7.9). If our base τ drops there too, that is the headroom, and a candidate ingredient for the 100–150K run (§8).
- Keep reporting OOD v3 (summarization τ 2.8, the weakest domain) next to the in-distribution test.

## 3. Protocol shared by every run

- **Data:** the 24K mixed store and its eval sets (validation 500, test 500, OOD v3 240), unchanged, so every arm pairs with MIX24K.
- **References, no rerun needed:**
  - 24K static r32 (KL): test τ 7.447;
  - 24K generator 1.1B (KL): test τ 7.469;
  - base: 7.251.
- **Two run lengths:**
  - **Screen:** 1 epoch, 1,333 updates, ≈ 1 h; validation only.
  - **Confirm:** 3 epochs, 4,000 updates, ≈ 3 h; validation + test + OOD, paired bootstrap CIs vs the references.
  - The paired CI on 500 rows is about ±0.016 τ. **A screen can only kill arms or flag effects of ≳ 0.03 τ.** Margins of the generator's size (+0.02) need a confirm, and finalists need seeds.
- **Logged for every arm:**
  - category-mean τ and pooled accepted-per-100-blocks;
  - per-slot accuracy;
  - fixes / breaks vs base (PERTURB churn);
  - held-out loss and plain KL to the target, to check the objective moves with τ;
  - trainable parameter count, split into encoder / trunk / heads;
  - ms per generated LoRA.
- **Generator diagnostics (X-pack), offline after every generator confirm (≈ 15 min):**

  | Diagnostic | What it shows |
  |---|---|
  | Own LoRA vs **mean-condition LoRA** | Value of the per-prompt part |
  | Own LoRA vs **bias-only LoRA** (head weights zeroed: the built-in static part) | How much is dynamic at all |
  | Own LoRA vs **category-centroid LoRA** | Router or more |
  | **PCA-k truncation** of the generated set, k = 0 / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 | Dynamic dimensions actually used |
  | D13 similarity | Collapse check |
  | M4 coefficient | Scale check |

- **"Learns as I want":** a generator passes if, with CIs:
  - own > its matched static and own > its mean-condition LoRA;
  - own ≥ its category centroid;
  - effective rank > 1, with no collapse during training;
  - OOD ≥ static.

## 6. Phase 2: generator size and architecture (≤ 500M), all with L\*

### 6.1 Why the generator is 1–2B, and the levers
- **Heads are ≈ 99% of the parameters:** (W + 1) × 4.26M at rank 32 on attention × 5 layers.
- **The head biases already form a static LoRA,** so generator = static + (part linear in the code z).
- **The rest is ≈ 12M,** and the 10.6M encoder dominates small designs. Encoder hidden 256 gives 5.3M; a fixed PCA gives 0.
- **Levers, in order of how much they cut:**
  1. the code width W per module;
  2. sharing heads across layers (per_type);
  3. emitted rank r (heads ∝ r);
  4. **generating the LoRA in chunks** with a shared network instead of one output neuron per factor entry.

### 6.2 Architecture at a fixed ≈ 280M (isolates *how* parameters are used)
All ≈ 280M (calculator in `$CLAUDE_JOB_DIR/tmp/g500.py`; encoder hidden 256, condition 32):

| ID | Design | Config | Params | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Current generator, narrow code | per_module, W 64, r32 | 282M | config only |
| H2 | Shared heads across layers, wider code | per_type, W 320, r32 (+ per-module bias, 4.3M) | 279M (+4) | config + per-module bias |
| H3 | **Factorized heads** (bottleneck): trunk W 512 → per-module code k 64 → factor basis | k = 64 | ≈ 290M | small; equals H1 plus a nonlinear trunk before the bottleneck |
| H4 | **Basis-LoRA mixture:** ΔW = LoRA₀ + Σᵢ cᵢ(x)·LoRAᵢ, LoRA₀ static, tiny router | N = 64, r32 | 273M + router | moderate; batches in vLLM as one rank-N·r LoRA with per-request scales (the nice-to-have) |
| H5 | **Chunked / token generator ("DFlash for weights"):** a small transformer reads the condition plus learned (module, layer, factor, row) query embeddings and emits each LoRA row (A rows of 4,096, B columns of 4,096 / 1,024) in parallel, one query per row; one shared output head d → 4,096 | d 512, 4–6 layers, ≈ 1,280 row-queries per LoRA | ≈ 20–40M (heads no longer scale with the factor count) | largest; the main size-breaker |
| H6 | Warm-started current generator: H1 with head biases initialized from the trained 24K static LoRA, dynamic weights at zero, 2–3× LR on the dynamic part | as H1 | 282M | small; reuse |

- **Cost:** 6 screens + 3 confirms of the top three ≈ 15 GPU-h.
- **Reading:** H1 vs H2 vs H3 asks whether the 1.1B could simply be narrower. H4 and H5 ask whether a different way of emitting ΔW helps, at the same or a far smaller size.

- **The size question:** where does the margin over static stop growing? X1 (free SVD truncation) predicts this before training.

### 6.4 Diffusion-style and reuse options (assessment)
- **H5 is the DFlash-like design:** it generates the LoRA's rows as parallel "tokens" from learned queries, the way DFlash denoises a block of masks in one pass. It is trained end-to-end through the drafter loss; no noise schedule is needed. It is also the design that breaks the size–output coupling.
- **A true diffusion or flow-matching generator over LoRA weights** (p-diff-style) needs a training set of target LoRAs and several sampling steps per request.
  - Its strength is modelling multimodal distributions over weights, but we need one point estimate per prompt.
  - The 24K generator uses ≈ 2.5 dimensions (D13).
  - **Not recommended now.** If wanted later, the cheap form is a flow model in H4's coefficient space or H3's code space (≤ 64 dimensions), distilled from a trained deterministic generator.
- **Reuse, ranked by value:**
  1. **X1:** SVD-compress the 1.1B generator. Free.
  2. **H6:** warm-start the static part from the trained static.
  3. **Drafter-feature conditioning:** pool the drafter's own layer outputs over the prompt (computed in serving anyway) instead of the 20,480-wide target summary. This removes the encoder. Category is decodable from every drafter layer (≥ 0.96). One screen, as H7 if Phase 2 has a winner.
  4. Distill a big generator's outputs into a small one with the existing `factor_loss` (`model.py:294`). This is also the C3 positive control.
